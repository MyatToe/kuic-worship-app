# Song sources

Where the songs added on 2026-10-15 come from, and why they may be published here.

## Public-domain hymns with lyrics and chords (139)

**Public-domain test:** every author, translator and tune composer died in 1955 or earlier (life + 70 years) and the text and tune were published before 1929, so they are public domain in the US, Korea and life+70 countries. Death years come from the Cyber Hymnal biographies (hymntime.com). Songs that failed the test were left out (see the end of this file).

**Lyrics:** the Cyber Hymnal page (punctuation and line breaks); where a stanza in the score is a different version of the text, the words printed in that score are used. Only stanzas found in the score are included.

**Chords:** worked out from the four-part harmony (soprano, alto, tenor, bass) of a published hymnal setting, beat by beat, then passing chords removed so a guitarist can read them. Source: the Open Hymnal Project score (ABC) where one exists — that score is also linked as sheet music (`{score:}`) when its setting is public domain — otherwise the Cyber Hymnal NoteWorthy score, which names the hymnal it was copied from. Where both exist the two harmonizations were compared syllable by syllable (agreement shown). A chord progression is not copyrightable; no arrangement text was copied. Each file says its source in `{c: Chords checked against …}`.

**Scores:** 42 new files in `songs/scores/` from the [Open Hymnal Project](http://openhymnal.org/) (each file states "copyright: public domain"), tidied the same way as the existing scores. Settings by arrangers who died after 1955 or are unknown (e.g. Ralph Vaughan Williams / *The English Hymnal* 1906, 1931 hymnals) are used for chords only, without the score.

## Chord-only charts for licensed songs (133)

**What they are:** for 133 of the catalog entries above, the file now carries a **chord-only chart** — section labels (Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Tag…) and the chords of each line, one chord per bar — with **no lyrics at all** (not even first words). Marked `{chords_only: true}`. A chord progression is generally not protected by copyright; the lyrics are, so they are still added by the church from its licensed copy (CCLI SongSelect). The projector shows only a title slide (title, writers, CCLI #).

**How they were checked:** each chart is in the file's original key and is the **consensus of two independent chord sites** (Ultimate Guitar — the highest-voted tab by the original artist — worshipchords.com, pnwchords.com and others). The pages were read by a script that keeps only section headings and chord lines and drops every lyric line, then transposed to the original key and compared section by section. Where the sites differed only in colour chords (sus, add9, slash bass) the simpler common form was used; a section that only one source had was left out; a song whose sources disagreed on the main progression was skipped. worshipchords.com and pnwchords.com sometimes carry the same transcription — then they counted as one source. Bar lengths follow the sources' line groupings and the song's meter; they are a band chart, not a transcription of the recording. Each file names its two sources in `{c: Chords checked against …}`.
